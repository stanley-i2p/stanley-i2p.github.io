I2P-native communication architecture

CommTools-I2P

A communication model built around direct I2P identities, strict relationship compartments, encrypted application sessions, and offline infrastructure that never needs to understand who is communicating or what is being said.

CommTools-I2P is the shared architecture and protocol family behind multiple interfaces. It is not a central service, account provider, or relay network.

The Model In Four Principles

Each layer has one narrow responsibility, and no infrastructure component is trusted with more information than it requires.

01

I2P is the transport

Communication uses cryptographic I2P destinations and SAM streams. Network location remains outside the application protocol.

02

Relationships are compartments

Persistent one-to-one contacts use separate identities and state. One profile does not become a universal identity across every relationship.

03

Protection continues above I2P

Peer checks, application-level encrypted framing, delivery state, and heartbeat monitoring form a session layer above anonymous transport.

04

Servers remain untrusted

Offline deaddrops store opaque encrypted blobs under derived keys. They provide availability, never identity or confidentiality.

Identity Compartmentalization

Long-term relationships do not share one global address. Temporary sessions, persistent one-to-one contacts, and groups occupy distinct identity spaces with separate trust and runtime state.

Communication Modes

The architecture uses different models where the security and availability requirements are genuinely different.

Live one-to-one

Direct when both peers are online

A profile-bound I2P stream connects the two endpoints directly. Persistent contacts can be locked and pinned with TOFU continuity checks.

Offline one-to-one

Encrypted mailboxes without accounts

Replicated deaddrop servers hold opaque blobs. Per-message keys and transient access identities minimize durable server-side metadata.

Live groups

Peer fan-out with separate identities

Group participants use dedicated group identities and direct member connections. Signed rosters keep membership authority separate from ordinary messaging.

Direct Live One-to-One Session

A live conversation combines I2P destination authenticity with profile-level peer validation and an encrypted application session. No CommTools-I2P relay is required while both peers are online.

Safer Persistent Contact Bootstrap

A disposable transient session can separate first contact from a long-term relationship. The initial coordination channel sees only a temporary address; persistent identities are exchanged later inside the private transient session, verified, locked to their profiles, and kept after the temporary sessions are discarded.

Offline Delivery Through Untrusted Deaddrops

Offline delivery is asynchronous and independent of the live stream. The sender replicates an encrypted opaque blob to several deaddrops, and the receiver retrieves it later. PUT and GET operations use transient I2P access identities, while the stable relationship remains inside the matching endpoint profiles.

Expected-Key Offline Retrieval

A receiver never searches a deaddrop or asks it to identify an account. Its local profile computes the exact mailbox keys expected for a bounded range of message indexes, queries ranked replicas, authenticates any returned blob, and advances its receive state only after a valid hit.

Decentralized Deaddrop Discovery

Deaddrop knowledge can spread during ordinary trusted one-to-one contacts instead of coming from a central directory or DHT. Peers merge useful entries, measure reliability locally, rank servers from their own results, and pass selected knowledge onward during later contacts.

Groups As Direct Fan-Out

A group is not represented by a provider-hosted room. A participant uses a dedicated group identity and sends the same message over separate direct member paths. Membership and roster authority remain group concerns, isolated from persistent one-to-one profiles and their offline state.

Sensitive Trust And Secrets Remain At The Endpoints

CommTools-I2P reduces infrastructure trust by separating transport, session security, offline availability, and local persistence.

External infrastructure may observe

  • Connection or mailbox access timing
  • Approximate encrypted traffic and blob sizes
  • Availability failures and retries visible locally

External infrastructure does not know

  • A central account, phone number, or email address
  • A global contact list or complete social graph
  • Message plaintext or offline shared secrets

The strongest remaining risks are endpoint compromise, incorrect first-contact trust decisions, implementation defects, denial of service, and broad timing analysis. The model minimizes network and service trust; it does not claim to protect a compromised device.

One Architecture, Multiple Interfaces

The protocol and security model are independent of a particular user interface. Implementations can serve different environments while preserving the same communication boundaries.
